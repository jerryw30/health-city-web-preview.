Understanding PMOS

PMOS is the updated name for the condition previously known as polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). The new terminology reflects the interconnected hormonal, metabolic, and ovarian features of the condition.

PMOS can affect women differently. Common experiences include:

Irregular, infrequent, or absent periods

Persistent acne or increased facial and body hair

Hair thinning or changes in hair growth

Insulin resistance or changes in blood sugar regulation

Weight changes or challenges with weight management

Difficulty identifying ovulation or conceiving

You don't need to experience all of these symptoms to have PMOS. And despite the previous name, ovarian cysts are not required for a diagnosis.

If you suspect you may have PMOS, a medical assessment is important. Naturopathic care can complement that process and support your overall health.

How I Can Support You

There isn't one diet, supplement, or lifestyle plan that works for every woman with PMOS. Your care should reflect your symptoms, health history, and priorities.

Depending on your individual needs, we may explore three key areas:

Nutrition and Metabolic Health

Nutrition is a key component of supporting metabolic health, particularly for women experiencing insulin resistance or changes in blood glucose regulation.

We'll look at your current eating patterns and identify practical ways to support balanced meals, adequate nourishment, energy, and sustainable habits — without unnecessary restriction or one-size-fits-all diets.

Hormonal and Menstrual Health

Irregular periods, acne, and changes in hair growth can be frustrating to navigate.

We'll consider your menstrual history, symptoms, and relevant health information to help you better understand your hormonal health and identify appropriate areas of support.

Stress, Sleep and Overall Wellbeing

Your health involves more than hormones and lab results.

Sleep, stress, emotional wellbeing, and daily routines can all influence how you feel and how manageable your health goals are. We'll consider these factors as part of a realistic, individualized plan.

Where appropriate, we can also discuss nutritional supplements and relevant testing. Recommendations are based on your individual circumstances rather than a standard PMOS protocol.

Your Care Should Reflect Your Goals

PMOS can look different at different stages of life. Your priorities may also change over time.

You may be looking to:

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PMOS the same as PCOS?

Yes. PMOS (polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome) is the updated name for the condition previously known as polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). You may still see both terms used during the transition.

Can you have PMOS without ovarian cysts?

Yes. Ovarian cysts are not required for a diagnosis. PMOS is assessed using established clinical criteria that consider menstrual and ovulatory function, androgen levels or signs of androgen excess, and ovarian morphology or other appropriate markers.

Does everyone with PMOS have insulin resistance?

Insulin resistance is extremely common and can be present in all body types, but it isn't a requisite for diagnosis. Metabolic health should be considered individually.

Can naturopathic care help with PMOS?

Naturopathic care may help those with PMOS through individualized nutrition, lifestyle, and supplement considerations where appropriate. The approach depends on your symptoms, health history, and goals.

Can I get pregnant if I have PMOS?

Yes. Many women with PMOS conceive, although irregular ovulation can make conception more challenging for some. If pregnancy is a goal, your care plan can take preconception health and cycle awareness into account alongside appropriate medical or fertility care.
