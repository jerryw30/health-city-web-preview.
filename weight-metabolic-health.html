A More Thoughtful Approach to Weight Loss—One That Looks at the Whole Picture

If you've been trying to lose weight but aren't seeing the results you expected, it can be frustrating to feel like your efforts aren't adding up.

Maybe you're eating less, exercising more and trying to make healthier choices, but your weight hasn't changed. Or perhaps your energy, appetite, cravings or body composition seem different than they used to.

There isn't always one explanation.

Sometimes hormones play a role. Changes associated with postpartum recovery, perimenopause, PMOS, thyroid function, insulin or leptin resistance can influence appetite, energy, metabolism and body composition. But sometimes the factors are more practical: your calorie intake may not consistently align with your goals, your meals may not be keeping you satisfied, your nutrition strategy may not suit your lifestyle, or your activity and recovery habits may need adjusting.

Stress, sleep, medications, health conditions and the demands of everyday life can also influence weight.

The goal isn't to assume your hormones are the problem—or to tell you to simply eat less and exercise more. It's to understand the factors influencing your weight and create a strategy that makes sense for your body, health and life.

Through virtual naturopathic care, I support women across Ontario with a personalized, evidence-informed approach to weight management.

Weight Loss Is More Complex Than Calories Alone—but the Fundamentals Still Matter

Body weight is influenced by several interconnected factors. While a sustained energy deficit is generally necessary for weight loss, maintaining one can be more challenging than it sounds.

Appetite, food choices, portion sizes, meal patterns, physical activity, sleep, stress and hormonal changes can all affect how easy or difficult it is to maintain consistent habits over time.

Rather than jumping straight to hormone testing or restrictive diets, we start by looking at the bigger picture.

1. Nutrition, Appetite & Eating Patterns

Sometimes the most useful place to start is with your everyday nutrition.

Are you eating enough protein and fibre? Are your meals satisfying? Are you going long periods without eating and then feeling overly hungry later? Are portions, snacks, beverages or weekend habits affecting your overall intake?

We look at your current eating patterns, food preferences, schedule and relationship with food to identify practical opportunities for change.

The focus is on building meals and routines that support fullness, energy, nutritional adequacy and consistency—not crash dieting or unnecessary restriction.

2. Hormones & Metabolic Health

Hormones can influence appetite, energy expenditure, fluid balance, body composition and how you feel throughout the day.

Depending on your symptoms and health history, relevant factors may include:

Thyroid function: An underactive thyroid can contribute to fatigue and weight changes, although it is only one possible factor.

Insulin and leptin sensitivity: Insulin or leptin resistance and blood sugar regulation may be relevant.

Reproductive hormones: PMOS, postpartum changes and perimenopause can influence appetite, body composition and weight distribution.

Other health factors: Certain medications and medical conditions can also affect weight.

When your history suggests a possible hormonal or metabolic contributor, appropriate assessment and laboratory testing may help clarify the picture. Testing is guided by your individual symptoms—not ordered automatically.

3. Stress, Sleep & Nervous System Health

Chronic stress and inadequate sleep can make healthy routines harder to maintain.

They may influence hunger and fullness cues, cravings, food choices, energy, motivation and recovery. Stress physiology can also interact with metabolic health, but cortisol is not automatically the reason someone is struggling to lose weight.

We consider your sleep quality, daily stressors, exercise demands and recovery habits, then identify realistic ways to support them.

4. Movement, Muscle & Recovery

Exercise is important for overall health, but more isn't always better.

Your current activity level, strength training, daily movement, recovery and consistency all matter. Building and maintaining muscle can support long-term metabolic health and physical function.

We'll consider what you're currently doing and whether your movement routine is appropriate for your goals, preferences and stage of life.

5. Postpartum & Life-Stage Changes

Pregnancy, postpartum recovery and the transition into perimenopause can bring changes in sleep, appetite, activity, body composition and hormonal patterns.

These transitions deserve a thoughtful approach—not pressure to return to a previous weight on an unrealistic timeline.

If you're postpartum, we consider your recovery, feeding goals where relevant, sleep, nutritional needs and overall health before discussing weight management. The priority is supporting your health while respecting what your body is navigating.

A Personalized Approach to Sustainable Weight Management

There is no single diet, supplement or testing panel that works for everyone. Your plan should reflect what is actually influencing your health and your ability to make sustainable changes.

Our approach may include:

A closer look at your current habits

We review your eating patterns, meal composition, activity, sleep, stress and health history to understand what's already working and where adjustments may help.

Individualized nutrition strategies

Depending on your needs, we may focus on protein and fibre intake, meal structure, portion awareness, food quality, appetite regulation and realistic consistency.

Relevant laboratory investigation

When clinically appropriate, we may consider thyroid markers, blood glucose and other metabolic or hormonal markers based on your symptoms and health history. Testing is selected with a clear purpose and interpreted in context.

Lifestyle and stress support

We consider sleep, recovery, movement and stress management as part of the bigger picture. The goal is to build routines that are achievable in your actual life.

Targeted nutritional and supplement support

When appropriate, supplements or other naturopathic recommendations may be incorporated to address identified nutritional needs or support a broader care plan. They are not a substitute for foundational habits or medical care when needed.

More Than a Number on the Scale

Weight can be one measure of progress, but it isn't the only one.

As we work toward your goals, we may also pay attention to:

More consistent energy throughout the day

Improved meal satisfaction and fewer episodes of intense hunger

Better sleep and recovery

Improved strength, fitness and physical confidence

Healthier blood sugar and metabolic markers, where relevant

More consistent eating and movement habits

A more sustainable relationship with food and your body

Progress looks different for everyone. We focus on meaningful, realistic changes rather than promising a particular number on the scale or a specific timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why am I not losing weight even though I'm eating healthy and exercising?

There can be several reasons. Healthy food choices don't always translate into a consistent energy deficit, and factors such as portion sizes, appetite, activity levels, sleep, stress, medications and hormonal or medical conditions can all play a role.

We start by understanding your current habits and health history rather than assuming there's one underlying cause.

Could my hormones be making it harder to lose weight?

They could be one contributing factor. Thyroid dysfunction, PMOS, insulin or leptin resistance and hormonal changes during postpartum recovery or perimenopause may influence weight and body composition. However, hormones aren't always the explanation. An individualized assessment can help determine whether further investigation is appropriate.

Do I need hormone or cortisol testing?

Not necessarily. Testing should be based on your symptoms, medical history and clinical assessment. In some cases, bloodwork may be helpful; in others, reviewing nutrition, activity, sleep and daily routines may be the more appropriate starting point.

Cortisol testing is not a routine requirement for weight management.

Can you help with postpartum weight changes?

We can discuss nutrition, energy, recovery and lifestyle factors in the context of your postpartum health. The timing and approach to weight management should account for recovery, nutritional needs, sleep and whether you're breastfeeding. Your care plan should never rely on aggressive calorie restriction especially if you’re breastfeeding your newborn but most women can lose excess weight during breast feeding by following a modest caloric deficit.

Do you offer virtual appointments?

Yes. Virtual naturopathic appointments are available to women across Ontario, allowing you to access individualized support from home.
