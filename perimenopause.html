Understanding Perimenopause

Perimenopause is the transition leading up to menopause, when ovarian hormone production and menstrual cycles begin to change.

Estrogen and progesterone levels can fluctuate throughout this stage. As a result, symptoms may come and go, change over time, or affect different areas of your health.

Some women notice subtle changes, while others experience symptoms that significantly affect their sleep, energy, mood and quality of life.

Common Perimenopause Symptoms

Perimenopause can look different for everyone, but common experiences include:

Sleep changes: Difficulty staying asleep, waking during the night or feeling unrefreshed in the morning.

Mood changes: Increased irritability, anxiety, mood fluctuations or feeling less emotionally resilient.

Changes in your menstrual cycle: Periods becoming heavier, lighter, shorter, longer or less predictable.

Hot flashes and night sweats: Sudden waves of heat or sweating that can interrupt sleep and daily activities.

Changes in energy and concentration: Fatigue, brain fog or difficulty focusing.

Changes in weight and body composition: Shifts in body composition or increased abdominal fat.

Other changes: Lower libido, vaginal dryness or discomfort, headaches, and changes in skin or hair.

Not every symptom is necessarily caused by perimenopause. Thyroid conditions, iron deficiency, sleep disorders and other health concerns can produce similar experiences, so it's important to consider the bigger picture.

How I Can Support You Through Perimenopause

There isn't one hormone-balancing protocol or lifestyle plan that works for every woman. Your care should reflect your symptoms, health history and priorities.

My approach considers several interconnected areas of health.

1. Nutrition and Metabolic Health

Your nutritional needs and body composition may change during the menopause transition.

We'll explore practical ways to support energy, nourishment, blood sugar regulation and metabolic health through balanced nutrition and sustainable habits.

Depending on your needs, this may include reviewing protein and fibre intake, meal patterns, nutritional adequacy and other factors that influence your overall health.

2. Hormonal and Menstrual Health

Changes in estrogen and progesterone can influence menstrual patterns, sleep, mood and other aspects of wellbeing.

We'll review your cycle history, symptoms and relevant health information to better understand what you're experiencing and identify appropriate areas of support.

Where indicated, this may include discussing laboratory investigations or coordinating with your other healthcare providers.

Bioidentical hormones may be a good therapeutic option.

3. Sleep, Stress and Nervous System Health

When sleep becomes disrupted and stress feels harder to manage, everyday life can become more demanding.

We'll consider your sleep patterns, daily routines, stressors and recovery habits, and explore practical strategies that support rest and overall wellbeing.

Nutritional supplements or botanical options may also be considered when appropriate, taking into account your health history, medications and individual needs.

What If You Want to Explore Hormone Therapy?

You don't have to choose between a completely natural approach and conventional medical care.

Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT), sometimes called hormone replacement therapy (HRT), is an evidence-based option that can be appropriate for some women experiencing perimenopause symptoms that significantly affect their quality of life.

Whether you're curious about hormone therapy, already using it, or prefer to explore non-hormonal options, your questions and preferences matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Am I too young for perimenopause?

Perimenopause most commonly begins in the 40s, although it can occur earlier. If you're experiencing changes in your periods, sleep or other symptoms, it's worth discussing them with your healthcare provider. Similar symptoms can have other causes, so it's important not to assume every change is hormonal.

Do I need hormone testing to know if I'm in perimenopause?

Not necessarily. For many women over 45, perimenopause can be identified based on symptoms and menstrual changes without hormone testing. Hormone levels fluctuate during this stage, so a single test may not accurately reflect what's happening. Testing may be appropriate in certain circumstances, particularly when symptoms occur at a younger age or another condition is suspected.

Is perimenopause the same as menopause?

No. Perimenopause is the transition leading up to menopause. Menopause is confirmed retrospectively after 12 consecutive months without a menstrual period, when there isn't another explanation for the absence of periods.

Can I still get pregnant during perimenopause?

Yes. Ovulation can still occur during perimenopause, even when periods become irregular. Pregnancy remains possible until menopause has been reached. If you don't want to become pregnant, contraception is still important during this transition.
